# Slideshow hook playbook — TikTok photo mode + Instagram carousels

*Created 2026-07-05. The reusable hook system for our two swipe-driven surfaces: TikTok
photo mode and Instagram carousels. This is NOT for video reels (different metric, different
mechanics). Pairs with `docs/voice-and-tone.md` (voice gate, every line), `docs/social-content-
calendar.md` (pillars + cadence) and `docs/content-strategy.md` (the "worth it" value bar). If
this doc and the voice canon ever disagree, the canon wins.*

> **What this doc is for:** slide 1 (the hook frame) and slide 2 (the "keep swiping" beat) do
> ~80% of a carousel's work. This gives us 15 fill-in-the-blank hook templates, each with an
> on-voice Luxury Catalog example built on a real data angle, and the metric it moves, so we
> stop writing cover slides from scratch and stop drifting off-voice.

---

## 0. Why the first two slides are the whole game (the research, dated + sourced)

The mechanics below are cross-niche findings from 2025-2026 carousel/photo-mode breakdowns.
None are broken out for secondhand-luxury specifically (nobody publishes that), so treat the
multipliers as directional and let our own saves/swipe-through be the tiebreaker.

- **Carousels out-earn other formats, and the save is the currency.** Reported ~10% engagement
  rate for IG carousels vs ~7% single image and ~6% Reels; carousels earn roughly 22-49% more
  saves than single images depending on the study (ranges disagree on size, agree on direction).
  (PostNitro, *Boost Carousel Swipe-Through Rate*, Dec 15 2025; Socialmon, *55 Viral Carousel
  Post Ideas*, Jul 2 2026, which explicitly presents these as a range, not a law.)
- **Slide 1 decides ~80% of swipe-through.** The cover is a contract, not a title card: it says
  exactly what you get if you swipe and what you lose if you don't. Clear beats clever every
  time on the cover, because the viewer is moving fast and won't decode wordplay. (PostNitro,
  Dec 2025; Socialmon, Jul 2026.)
- **The curiosity gap is the engine.** Hint at a specific, finished payoff; withhold the "how"
  or the "which one" until they swipe. Give the whole idea away on slide 1 and you've thrown
  away the swipe, the save, and the re-serve. (PostNitro, Dec 2025; Socialmon, Jul 2026;
  Instagram creator lesson set @ reel DFsavqfI006, "13 hooks that stop the scroll," 2025.)
- **The IG "second-chance" re-serve.** If someone scrolls past your cover, Instagram can re-show
  the post starting on a later slide. So a strong self-contained beat in the MIDDLE (a striking
  number, a before/after) can hook a viewer who ignored slide 1. Don't hoard your best proof for
  the last slide. (Socialmon, Jul 2026; multiple 2026 carousel guides.)
- **Slide count matches depth:** 2-4 slides for one sharp idea (myth + correction, one
  comparison), 5-7 for an explainer, 8-10 for a deep dive. Filler slides tank completion.
  (PostNitro, Dec 2025.) **For us: 3-6 is the default.** Short-form is ONE idea, one hook, one
  takeaway (`voice-and-tone.md` §7b) — the full ranking/list is the article we route to.
- **Text length:** headline ~5 words or fewer on the cover, body text large enough to read on a
  phone at arm's length (roughly 30pt-equivalent), one idea per slide, high contrast. (OctoSpark,
  *How to Make Instagram Carousel*, 2025; Futuristic Marketing, *Carousel Design Guide*, 2025.)
- **A number in the hook out-pulls a general claim** ("this one change added 40% more saves"
  beats "get more saves"). Proof travels further than opinion, and gets screenshotted (a
  screenshot is a save that walks into a DM). (Socialmon, Jul 2026; PostNitro, Dec 2025.)
- **The action follows the format:** framework/reference carousels earn the most SAVES; myth-bust
  and hot-take carousels earn the most COMMENTS/shares; relatable "which one are you" earns the
  most TAGS/shares (widest reach, weakest save). Decide the action before you build. (Socialmon,
  Jul 2026.)
- **Add a literal swipe cue.** "Swipe" prompts + a bottom-right arrow measurably lift engagement
  (one 2022 study: 1.83% -> 2.00% just from the word). Cheap, always do it. (Socialmon, Jul 2026.)

### What slide 2 has to do (earn the swipe it just bought)
Slide 1 opens the loop; **slide 2 must widen it, not close it.** The failure mode is resolving
the tension on slide 2 (then there's no reason to keep going, and you lose the re-serve). The
moves that work:
- **Contrast / "but":** slide 1 states the expectation, slide 2 flips it ("here's the part that
  breaks the rule"). Do NOT state the naive premise in our own voice (see §1 gate) — attribute it.
- **Raise the stakes with a number:** slide 1 asks, slide 2 drops the surprising figure that
  makes the rest matter, then defers the "why" or the "which."
- **Half-thought hand-off:** end slide 2 on a sentence the next slide completes ("and the fix
  isn't what you'd think"). The reader doesn't decide to swipe, they just keep going.
- **"Guess before you swipe":** slide 2 poses the choice (A or B?) and holds the answer.

## 1. The locked voice + strategy gates (every hook passes these, no exceptions)

These are owner-locked. A hook that fails any one does not ship, no matter how well it'd perform.

1. **Value = "is it worth it, and worth it to *you*"** (informed choice over gut). We help people
   decide, we don't judge the choice. (`content-strategy.md` §48; voice §1.)
2. **NO haggle / overpaid / negotiate-it-down framing.** Most resale is fixed-price, so asking ≈
   sold on the fixed-price platforms; "you overpaid / talk them down" is off-strategy and was
   corrected by the owner. Never build a hook on it. Our money angle is *what it actually sells
   for* and *what holds value*, not *how to lowball*.
3. **Never assert a naive premise in our own flat voice, then overturn it.** Attribute the
   assumption we're about to break: "you'd think…," "the rule says…," "…right?," "everyone says…"
   Then turn it. (voice §7 + §7b, locked 2026-06-30.)
4. **Numbers are dated estimates, hedged.** Every price/resale figure is "asking not sold,
   condition varies, an estimate of the market not an appraisal of one bag," carried as a short
   human note (or on the chart slide). Never a verdict, never invented, sourced with its date.
   (voice §7b; `content-strategy.md`.)
5. **No em dashes** (sole exception: the verbatim tagline). Sentence case. Capital after a colon.
   No hype superlatives in the brand voice (stunning/iconic/must-have), no AI-slop tells (voice §8).
6. **Lead with the bag visually.** Campaign cutouts + first-party stills are cleared for social.
   Pair every model name with the bag shown or a 3-word descriptor, because casual buyers know
   bags by look, not model name (Coach Tabby is the rare exception). (voice §7b, locked 2026-06-30.)
7. **Value-first, not asset-first.** Start from the real, often counterintuitive truth for Maya /
   Jordan; source the asset to fit. Never reverse-engineer a hook from the photos we happen to have.
8. **Every post routes to a live page, UTM-tagged** (`utm-conventions.md`). Default CTA
   pre-monetization is the taste quiz or save-the-bag; buy/sell/rent links are the passive layer.

## 2. How to build a slideshow post (the 6-step drill)

1. **Pick the ONE truth** (a real, counterintuitive Luxury Catalog data angle, §4 list below).
2. **Pick the action it should earn** (save / comment / share / tag) — that picks the hook family.
3. **Write slide 1 from a template below.** Bag on screen. Headline <=5 words of promise +
   curiosity gap. Add the swipe cue.
4. **Write slide 2 to widen the loop** (contrast / number / half-thought), never to resolve it.
5. **Middle slides:** one idea each; seed one self-contained proof beat mid-deck (for the re-serve).
6. **Last slide:** the payoff + the CTA ladder rung (quiz / save / read the full data at the link).
   Verify the destination is live and UTM-tag it. Ship 2-3 rotating slide-1 variants to A/B.

## 3. The pattern quick-map (which family for which job)

| Want them to… | Use these patterns |
|---|---|
| **Save** (reference/decision value) | Price Reveal, The Paradox, Decision Fork, Report Card, The Checklist Tell |
| **Comment / share** (hot take, myth) | Myth-Bust, The Contrarian Take, "You'd Think", The Receipt |
| **Tag / share** (reach) | Which One Are You, The Callout |
| **Swipe deep then save** (open loop) | The Withheld Winner, The Two-Bag Face-Off, The "Wait For It" |

## 4. The 15 hook templates

Each: **(a) pattern name · (b) template · (c) on-voice example on a real data angle · (d) metric
it moves.** Examples are drafted to voice; any live number must be re-confirmed + dated before it
ships (they're shown here as the shape, not a locked figure).

Our recurring data angles: the **size-price paradox** (smaller bag, higher price), **most-wanted
vs most-expensive**, the **thrift/Coach wedge**, **holds-value vs doesn't**, **taste / worth-it-
to-you**, the **modern-life report card** (the flagship POV).

---

**1. The Price Reveal** *(number-in-the-hook, curiosity gap)*
- **Template:** *Slide 1:* "[Bag people underrate], shown. Guess what it actually sells for."
  *Slide 2:* "You'd expect [low anchor]. It's [surprising figure] (asking, [month year])."
- **Example:** S1: "The thrifted Coach Tabby. Guess what it really sells for." (Tabby on screen.)
  S2: "You'd expect thrift-bin money. Recent asking sits well above that, condition depending
  ([month] 2026)." → routes to the Tabby resale article, chart slide carries the n + hedge.
- **Moves:** saves + outbound resale clicks. (The wedge; buyer-side honest money, never lowball.)

**2. The Paradox** *(counterintuitive truth, "you'd think" framing)*
- **Template:** *S1:* "The [smaller/cheaper-looking] one costs more. Here's the bag." *S2:* "The
  rule says small = less. These two say otherwise."
- **Example:** S1: "The smaller Lady Dior can cost more than the big one." (Two sizes side by
  side.) S2: "You'd think a smaller bag costs less. With this one, size and price run backwards."
  → `/articles/does-a-smaller-bag-cost-more`.
- **Moves:** saves + swipe-through (the paradox pulls to the "why"). Highest-share of our angles.

**3. The Decision Fork** *(guess-before-you-swipe, worth-it-to-you)*
- **Template:** *S1:* "[Bag A] or [Bag B]? Same money. Pick before you swipe." *S2:* "One [real
  trade-off]. One [other trade-off]. Neither is the 'right' answer, it's which fits you."
- **Example:** S1: "Birkin or Kelly? Same five figures. Pick before you swipe." (Both shown.)
  S2: "One opens fast and flops when it's unlocked. One locks tight and slows you down. Depends
  on your day." → `/articles/birkin-vs-kelly-which-hermes-bag`.
- **Moves:** saves + comments (people argue their pick) + quiz starts (which fits *you*).

**4. Myth-Bust** *(attributed premise, then the turn)*
- **Template:** *S1:* "'[Common belief]' … the tell that proves it wrong." *S2:* "Here's where
  that belief comes from. Here's what actually decides it."
- **Example:** S1: "'Imperfect stitching means it's fake.' Not always." (Flap detail shown.)
  S2: "The rule sounds safe. But older lambskin flaps came out of the atelier with quirks. Here's
  what a real red flag looks like instead." (auth-standard gate: markers to check, not a verdict)
  → `/articles/caviar-vs-lambskin-chanel-flap`.
- **Moves:** comments + shares + saves. (De-gatekeeping energy; the highest-comment family.)

**5. The Contrarian Take** *(hot take, "my take, no hate")*
- **Template:** *S1:* "[Loved thing] is overrated. My take, no hate." *S2:* "Loving it is valid.
  Here's the case for looking twice before you spend."
- **Example:** S1: "The Classic Flap might be overrated. My take, no hate." (Flap shown.) S2:
  "It's beautiful and it holds value. But for how you actually carry a bag, here's what it costs
  you." → `/articles/is-the-chanel-classic-flap-worth-it`.
- **Moves:** comments + shares (agree/disagree both engage). Founder-persona can voice the "my."

**6. The Withheld Winner** *(open loop, ranked payoff held to the end)*
- **Template:** *S1:* "[N] [category] bags, ranked by [real metric]. #1 surprised us." *S2:*
  "Start at the bottom. The order isn't what the price tags predict."
- **Example:** S1: "Which luxury bags actually work for modern life? Ranked." (report-card grid.)
  S2: "You'd assume the priciest wins. It doesn't. The holy trinity all miss the same thing."
  → flagship report-card article (build) / interim `/quiz`. Hold the Fendi Peekaboo win to the end.
- **Moves:** swipe-through (deep) + saves. Anchors the flagship POV series.

**7. The Two-Bag Face-Off** *(single surprising comparison)*
- **Template:** *S1:* "Two [same brand] bags. One's worth [X]x the other." *S2:* "Same house,
  same shelf. The gap isn't about which looks more expensive."
- **Example:** S1: "Two Coach bags. One resells for triple the other." (Tabby vs Rogue shown.)
  S2: "The trendy one and the quiet workhorse. Demand, not craftsmanship, sets the spread ([month]
  2026)." → the Coach resale article. (Chart slide carries the hedge.)
- **Moves:** saves + outbound clicks. The wedge, in a single comparison (not a 7-bag list).

**8. The Report Card** *(named examples, right-vs-wrong mechanism)*
- **Template:** *S1:* "[Category] doing it right vs doing it wrong. Grades inside." *S2:* "One
  fixes [real problem]. One ignores the thing that runs your life."
- **Example:** S1: "Luxury bags: modern-life report card." (Peekaboo A+, Birkin D shown.) S2:
  "The Fendi Peekaboo locks your phone away safe. The Birkin still has no crossbody strap." →
  flagship article / brand page. (Verify Peekaboo bar/divider/lock mechanics first.)
- **Moves:** saves + shares. The ownable thesis no competitor holds.

**9. The Checklist Tell** *(reference value, "save this")*
- **Template:** *S1:* "The [N]-second [real/fake or worth-it] check. Save this." *S2:* "Start
  here, most people skip it: [the one tell]."
- **Example:** S1: "The 10-second real-or-fake Coach check. Save it." (creed stamp shown.) S2:
  "Start with the creed stamp, not the logo. Here's what a real one reads like." (markers to
  check, not a verdict) → `/articles/how-to-authenticate-a-coach-bag`.
- **Moves:** saves (highest save-rate family) + return visits. Evergreen, keeps earning.

**10. "You'd Think"** *(attributed-assumption cold open)*
- **Template:** *S1:* "You'd think [common assumption]." *S2:* "[The bag/data that says
  otherwise], and it's not close."
- **Example:** S1: "You'd think the priciest bag holds value best." S2: "Depends on the house.
  One climbs on the resale floor, one leans on the retail hikes. Two opposite ways to hold value."
  → `/articles/chanel-vs-hermes-two-ways-to-hold-value`.
- **Moves:** swipe-through + saves. The cleanest way to obey the no-naive-premise rule.

**11. The Receipt** *(proof-forward, screenshot bait)*
- **Template:** *S1:* "Here's what [bag] actually did on resale. Receipts inside." *S2:* "Not a
  vibe, a number: [figure], [month year], [n on the chart]."
- **Example:** S1: "Did the emerald-green bag hold value? The receipts." (bag shown.) S2: "The
  honest answer isn't about the market, it's about how one bag got carried. Here's the read."
  (behavior not market; estimate not appraisal) → `/articles/are-designer-bags-a-good-investment`.
- **Moves:** saves + screenshots (private shares) + outbound clicks. Seed this beat mid-deck too.

**12. Which One Are You** *(identity, tag-a-friend, reach)*
- **Template:** *S1:* "Which [category] buyer are you? [A], [B], or [C]?" *S2:* "[Type A]:
  [one-line read]. Tag the friend who's [type]."
- **Example:** S1: "Which bag buyer are you: the hunter, the collector, or the one-good-bag
  minimalist?" (three bags shown.) S2: "The hunter flips Coach at Goodwill. Tag your hunter."
  → `/quiz`.
- **Moves:** tags + shares (widest reach) + quiz starts. Weakest saver, so follow it with a
  reference post (#9/#11) a few days later so new eyes have something to keep.

**13. The Callout** *(gentle, aimed at the lazy advice not a person)*
- **Template:** *S1:* "Stop [common over-do]. Do [better thing] instead." *S2:* "Here's why the
  usual advice quietly costs you."
- **Example:** S1: "Stop over-caring your leather. Store it better instead." (bag on a shelf, off
  the ground.) S2: "Most damage isn't dryness, it's storage. Even the old saying, a bag on the
  floor loses your money, is really about germs." (folklore attributed; source leather pros first)
  → care/storage article (build) / caviar-vs-lambskin interim.
- **Moves:** comments + saves. Southern-folklore hook is personal + funny + practical.

**14. The "Wait For It"** *(delayed payoff, deferred reveal)*
- **Template:** *S1:* "The bag [experts / the resale floor] quietly rate highest. Swipe for it."
  *S2:* "It's not the loudest name. It's the one that [does the real thing]." *(reveal last)*
- **Example:** S1: "The most slept-on Hermès isn't the Birkin. Swipe." (silhouette teased,
  not named.) S2: "It's roomy, it's crossbody, and it doesn't announce itself." (24/24 revealed
  on the final slide) → `/brand/4` (Hermès) / 24/24 bag page. Underrated-pick, her stills ready.
- **Moves:** swipe-through (deep) + saves + follows (POV travels).

**15. The Rent-First Frame** *(closest-to-money, worth-it-to-you)*
- **Template:** *S1:* "Before you buy [aspirational bag], rent it. Here's the math." *S2:* "Not
  'can't afford it.' It's 'find out if you'll actually carry it' before five figures."
- **Example:** S1: "Before the Birkin, rent one. Here's the honest math." (bag shown.) S2: "A
  month with it tells you more than any review. Then decide if it's worth it to you." →
  `/articles/should-you-rent-or-buy-a-designer-bag` (Vivrelle rental affiliate, closest-to-money).
- **Moves:** outbound rental clicks (the near-term money lane) + saves + quiz starts.

## 5. Slide-2 swipe-earners (the reusable second-beat lines)

Drop-in slide-2 patterns that widen the loop instead of closing it. Attribute any naive premise.

- **Contrast:** "You'd expect [X]. It's [Y]." / "Same [house/price/shelf]. The gap isn't [obvious cause]."
- **Number defer:** "[Surprising figure] ([month year]). The why is the interesting part."
- **Half-thought:** "…and the one that wins isn't the one you'd guess." (next slide completes it)
- **Guess:** "[A] or [B]? Hold your answer. Swipe." 
- **Stakes:** "This is the part that decides whether it's worth it to you."

Never on slide 2: the full answer, a bald "should," a hype superlative, an un-hedged number.

## 6. Pre-ship checklist (run before staging)

- [ ] Slide 1 makes a specific promise + curiosity gap, <=5-word headline, bag on screen, swipe cue.
- [ ] Slide 2 WIDENS the loop (contrast/number/half-thought), doesn't resolve it.
- [ ] One self-contained proof beat seeded mid-deck (for the IG re-serve).
- [ ] 3-6 slides, one idea per slide; the full list/ranking lives in the article we route to.
- [ ] No naive premise asserted in our voice (attributed), no haggle/overpaid framing.
- [ ] Numbers dated + hedged (asking not sold, estimate not appraisal); nothing invented; auth = markers not verdict.
- [ ] No em dashes, sentence case, capital after colon, no hype superlatives, no §8 AI tells.
- [ ] Destination page is LIVE + UTM-tagged; CTA rung set (quiz/save default pre-monetization).
- [ ] 2-3 rotating slide-1 variants for A/B.
- [ ] Named the metric this post moves (save / comment / share / outbound / quiz).
