# Content requirements: scripts + articles

*The owner's rules for every spoken script and every published article, consolidated from
her edit passes of 2026-07-02 (diaper + Chanel kits). These are REQUIREMENTS, not
suggestions. Every script is checked against the full list before it reaches a kit's
TELEPROMPTER section; every article is checked before publish. Supersedes the scattered
notes in content-kit-buildout.md.*

---

## 0. The worth-it bar — a take must earn its script (owner rule)

Before ANY take is drafted, name its payload in one sentence: the thing the viewer can
DO, decide, or stop believing after watching. No passing sentence, no script option.
The sentence passes only if all three hold:

1. **It's news to the target viewer.** Not a restatement of the hook, not what the
   comments we've read already say to each other.
2. **It's usable without leaving the couch.** A move ("offer near the sold number, walk
   if they won't come close"), a decision question ("does the bag have to stay your bag
   after diapers?"), or a corrected assumption ("the gucci baby bag was never made for
   babies"). "Go look it up" / "check my site" is a CTA, never a payload.
3. **She'd send it to a friend who asked.** If the honest reaction to the payload
   sentence is "obviously" or "so what," the idea goes back to the pipeline (📊
   Data-tied), not to the teleprompter.

Origin: the diaper Take-3 lesson. The numbers were real and verified, but "don't pay the
sticker, look up the sold price" wasn't worth a video until it became a negotiation play.
Data earns the video only when it's attached to a move.

**Corollary (Chanel Take-1 lesson):** when honest scoping guts a hook (the year-naming
spine collapsed under its own caveats), don't prop the hook up with qualifiers. Recenter
the take on the purpose the data carries at full strength; for us that's usually the
prices nobody else has. The weak spine can survive as one light beat inside the stronger
video.

## 1. Truth mechanics (scripts AND articles)

1.1 **Hook verbs must be literally true.** "Renamed" when the truth is "started naming
    new lines by year" is misleading at best; drama never buys a false verb.
1.2 **No totality claims** ("every", "all", "complete", "nobody", "everyone") unless
    literally true against our own catalog or data. Scope honestly and turn the scope
    into receipts: "not every bag the house ever made, I track more than a hundred
    Chanel styles, but the ones behind the searches." (Caught twice in one day: "Every
    Chanel bag type" and "each new line is named for the year" while the catalog held
    the named Souplissimo and Kelly Shopper, and the address-named 31.)
1.3 **Hedges decay.** "Creators report" buys time, not permanence. Before recording or
    publishing, any hedged fact old enough to be documented gets confirmed with an
    anchor or cut. A March launch may not still be "reported" in July.
1.4 **A comparison must carry its meaning where it's made.** Never imply a cautionary
    tale ("headed the way of the Boy") without saying what happened, grounded in our
    data ("more preloved Boys than Classic Flaps in my comps, at half the price").
1.5 **Two-futures structure.** When a take or section weighs outcomes, EACH outcome gets
    its own data description, symmetric: the Flap path (price holds under huge supply),
    the Boy path (supply stayed, buzz didn't), then the subject's current position as
    the open question. Never one path asserted and the other decoded.
1.6 **A "moment" claim carries its why in the same breath.** Never "everyone latched
    onto it" without what changed ("a new creative director's first bag is the signal of
    where the house goes next").
1.7 **Name collisions get disambiguated explicitly.** When a new release shares a name
    with an existing size or line (Blazy's Maxi Flap vs the Classic Flap's maxi size),
    say which is which in one sentence, and never sit one bag's comps under the other
    bag's story.
1.8 **People are identified on first mention**: "Jennie from Blackpink", "Kendall
    Jenner". A new fan never has to google a name.
1.9 **Only verified, dated numbers.** Unfilled figures stay as [DATA: spec] slots; a
    script with a [DATA] slot is not teleprompter-eligible; an article with one is not
    publishable. [VERIFY]-flagged claims are spoken as hedges or cut. The
    twenty-thousand-prices claim stays verified (57K+ price rows as of 2026-07-02;
    re-check before raising the spoken number) and appears at most once per video.

## 2. Voice and stance (scripts)

2.1 **I, never we.** She is one person, not an org. Written on-site copy may use brand
    voice; scripts are I/my.
2.2 **Friend, not brand.** Never recite a product's virtues in her own words (brochure).
    Start from the viewer's worry, attribute brand claims AS the brand's ("Goyard's own
    line about durability is... so they say"), then the nobody-tells-you truth from the
    buyer's side.
2.3 **Opinions are labeled mine.** "My take, not a rule." Value = my estimate from
    comps, never an appraisal; markers to check, never verdicts.
2.4 **Hooks are a benefit, never a gotcha**: "a number you'll want to hear about", not
    "a number nobody talks about". Surprise = "you might be surprised to learn".
2.5 **Advice is a MOVE, not homework.** Never end on "look up the price". Give the
    negotiation play (offer button, start near the sold number, walk away, supply =
    leverage) or the buying angle (condition arbitrage). A payoff may also be the
    decision QUESTION the viewer should ask themselves.

## 3. Structure (scripts)

3.1 **The hook names the search intent in sentence one**, especially when the intent
    differs from the bag.
3.2 **One plain payoff, sayable in one breath.** No symmetrical if/if constructions; no
    finance jargon.
3.3 **One register per take.** An orientation take ("welcome to Chanel in 2026") stays
    evergreen: names, systems, classics, prices. News blips (a launch, a drop date) live
    in the kit's trend take, where they read right instead of too short-form against
    standing facts.
3.4 **Credibility integrated at the data moment, never appended as identity.** The site
    mention attaches as a CLAUSE where the first number appears. A closing receipts line
    WITHOUT the name is good in list/countdown takes ("By the way, every number you just
    heard comes from the twenty thousand plus real prices I track at Luxury Catalog dot
    com"). "I'm Arielle" appears AT MOST ONCE PER SERIES, in the opener; episodes carry
    credibility through the track-clause and site mention alone.
3.5 **Every script ends one of two ways:** a specific question for the comments, OR the
    site call in the owner's formula: "Just search <key> on the site to read more. Link
    in bio." The trailing "Link in bio." (or "Link's in my bio.") is a SECONDARY tag,
    valid because the bio lands on /social; never the only CTA, never on question
    endings. The key comes from the routing registry (docs/social-routing.md); a series
    shares its article's key; new kits default to the article's natural topic words.
3.6 **Ranked/countdown takes**: count down ("In fourth place... And number one"), one
    concrete upside and ONE honest downside per item, a price spoken for ONE anchor item
    only (captions carry the rest with date + n), close on the receipts line + site call.
3.7 **Size runs and lineups get a visual**: a spoken list of sizes is a data-on-screen
    moment (cue it in the kit plan), and the article carries the matching diagram
    ([diagram: chanel-flap-sizes] pattern).

## 4. Language (scripts)

4.1 **Contractions always.** Never "is not" / "cannot" / "it is".
4.2 **Numbers spoken in full with units**: "fourteen hundred and ninety-five dollars".
4.3 **A bag model always carries its full house name, every mention**; a list may share
    one brand mention; never "LV" spoken. First jargon mention gets the plain spell-out.
4.4 **Complete sentences; no clipped fragments.** Soften absolutes. Finish every
    metaphor. Plain idioms over writerly ones. Less drama. No AI rhetorical tics ("And
    honestly?", "Let's be real"); full blacklist in voice-and-tone.md §8.
4.5 **One hedge per script maximum**, spoken naturally. Don't stack "estimate not
    appraisal" onto "dated comps".
4.6 **No em dashes** (tagline-only exception; it never appears in scripts).
4.7 **No internal jargon in speech**: "my comps", never "dated comps"; "the full info /
    the full article", never "the read". Dates and ns live in captions.
4.8 **Ownership voice**: "on my site", "I added... on my site", "Link's in my bio". Say
    the full domain ("Luxury Catalog dot com") at the credibility or closing site
    mention. Inline stage directions in parens ("(tea)") are the owner's and stay.

## 5. Logistics (scripts)

5.1 **No bags in hand.** Talking-head, green-screen, or data-on-screen only.
5.2 **Single source of truth + lean kit pages**: a kit page holds exactly one plan block
    and the 🎬 TELEPROMPTER (+ 📋 CAPTIONS + TAGS). Draft/annotated scripts live only in
    the repo. SYNC DIRECTION (learned the hard way, twice): the owner edits ON the
    Notion page and ON the live site, so page and site are canon; mirror her text back
    to the repo after every owner pass; never push repo text over hers without diffing.
    Article seeds never overwrite a live body without --force-body.
5.3 **Every take ships as a POST PACKAGE**: script + caption + tag set. The caption
    carries the target search phrase in plain text, the date + n for any number spoken,
    and the site pointer with the search term. Tags: 4-6, observed tags only, never
    invented, never trap tags.

## 6. Article rules (the same lessons, page form)

6.1 Truth mechanics §1 apply verbatim: literal verbs, no totality, hedge decay,
    comparisons carry meaning, two-futures symmetry, moment-why, name-collision
    disambiguation, identified people, verified dated numbers with n inline.
6.2 **An explainer of a change shows before AND after**: what stayed (the named
    classics) and what's new (the year-named lines), so new fans leave oriented, not
    corrected in the comments ("The names, before and after" section pattern).
6.3 **Size runs, lineups, and rankings get a diagram/chart component**, built on OUR
    data (order + asking medians + n; thin samples labeled), registered in the article
    renderer. Never invented dimensions; no logos.
6.4 **One bag's comps never sit under another bag's story.** If a section is about the
    new Maxi Flap, vintage Classic Flap maxi comps appear only as an explicitly labeled
    "budget route to the look."
6.5 **Numbers as bullets, n and date in parentheses, key takeaway as a callout (>)**,
    data-hedge note at the foot, no walls of text (voice-and-tone.md §7b).
6.6 **Scope notes are receipts**: "we track more than a hundred Chanel styles; these are
    the ones filling feeds."

## The pre-record / pre-publish checklist

- [ ] Worth-it bar: payload named in one sentence; a usable move / decision / correction
- [ ] Hook verb literally true; intent named in sentence one; benefit, not gotcha
- [ ] No totality claims; scope turned into receipts
- [ ] No stale hedges (anything documentable is confirmed with an anchor or cut)
- [ ] Comparisons and "moments" carry their meaning/why; name collisions disambiguated
- [ ] People identified on first mention
- [ ] I-voice; no brochure lines; opinions labeled mine; advice is a move
- [ ] One register per take; credibility at the data moment; name once per series max
- [ ] Ends on a comments question or "Just search <key>..." + bio tag
- [ ] Contractions, full spoken numbers with units, full house names, no fragments, no
      tics, max one hedge, no em dashes, no internal jargon
- [ ] Size run/lineup/ranking has its visual (script cue + article diagram)
- [ ] No [DATA]/[VERIFY] remaining; post package complete (caption carries date + n)
