# Chanel Classic Flap (11.12 / A01112) — size names and dimensions by era

*Archivist research, run 2026-07-26. Historical dimension archive for the most contentious sizing
in the catalog. Companion docs: `classic-flap-production-matrix.md` (the production/selector canon),
`chanel-flap-reseller-decode.md` (how each reseller labels the flaps),
`seasonal-archive/chanel.md` (season codes and the no-official-colour-name rule).*

**All measurements in this document are centimetres, stated Width x Height x Depth (W x H x D)**,
converted where a source published inches. Where a source used a different axis order I say so on
the row. Nothing here is interpolated. Where an era has no measurement, the row says
**not documented** and stays empty.

---

## The headline (read this first)

**A stated size word cannot resolve an era. A *measured* dimension can, but only for the two big
sizes.** The Medium and Small have not meaningfully changed since at least 1997, so their
dimensions carry zero era signal. The Jumbo and the Maxi *have* changed, by roughly 2 to 3 cm, and
that change is resolvable — but only against a real measurement, never against a number copied out
of a reseller size guide, because the spread *between published guides* is as large as the real
era difference.

The reliable era gate for the big sizes is not a dimension at all. It is **flap count**: the Jumbo
and Maxi were **single-flap until 2010** and **double-flap from 2010** (Boutique Patina via PurseBop,
2022-09-01). That is a binary, and it is the single most useful fact in this document.

Second headline: **"Large" and "Jumbo" are the same bag.** Chanel's own boutique/site word is
**Large**; "Jumbo" is the collector and reseller word. If the catalog is treating them as two sizes,
that is a data bug, not a real distinction.

## 3. Table B — the documented size and construction changes, in order

Every item below has a source. Nothing is inferred from a gap.

| When | What changed | Source | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|
| **~1992** | **Maxi introduced first**, at 13 inches, with oversized 24k-gold-plated CC turnlock, chunky chains and washer-sized screw-in grommets. Lambskin diamond quilt; the caviar version is slightly larger and chevron only. | Boutique Patina via PurseBop, 2022-09-01 | medium |
| **1993** | **Jumbo introduced**, 12 inches, **vertical Mademoiselle quilting only**, caviar and lambskin. | same | medium |
| **1994** | Jumbo becomes available in **classic diamond quilting**; this run continues to 1998. Most vintage caviar jumbos date to 1995. | same | medium |
| **1996–1997** | A one-year **"intermediary" Jumbo**: thinner chain, an in-between-size CC turnlock, thinner medium-style grommets, black only with a **black** interior (not the usual burgundy). Produced alongside the oversized-CC jumbo. | same | medium |
| **1998** | Jumbo CC turnlock **shrinks** to the same size used on the medium, small and mini. Chunky chain and washer grommets retained. This run lasts about six years. | same | medium |
| **1999** | **Silver hardware** introduced on the Jumbo. | same | medium |
| **2005** | Jumbo **reproportioned into roughly today's shape**: the flap edge drops to about two-thirds down the bag, the CC turnlock drops with it and grows slightly, the grommets thin out. | same | medium |
| **2008** | **24-carat gold plating discontinued** on hardware. | Rebag *The Vault* | medium |
| **2010** | **The single biggest era marker: the second ("double") flap is added to the Jumbo and the Maxi.** Grommet layout changes from one row of four to two rows of two. Slightly boxier profile. Accompanied by a roughly $900 price rise. | Boutique Patina via PurseBop, 2022-09-01 | medium |
| **2010** | **East-West discontinued.** | Rebag *The Vault* | medium |
| **2018** | Chanel announces it will stop using **exotic skins**. | Rebag *The Vault* | medium |
| **June 2020** | **chanel.com's published dimensions shrink**: the M/L goes from 6.3 x 10.2 x 2.8 in to 6.1 x 10 x 2.6 in on the US site, and the Canadian site drops 0.5 cm on every axis in the same week. The same reduction is reported on the small, the mini and the WOC. **The European site did not change at that date.** | PurseForum thread 1029368, 2020-06-06 | medium as a *spec* change, **unproven as a physical change** |
| **Spring 2021** | The Classic Flap is **officially renamed 11.12** (after the Medium's style code A01112); all sizes now carry that name. | Rebag *The Vault* | high |
| **date unknown** | **Extra Mini discontinued.** | Miss Bugis, 2020-07-02 | medium, no date |

**On the June 2020 change — the important caveat.** What is documented is that *chanel.com's
published figures* changed, regionally and not simultaneously. Nobody in the thread measured a bag.
A spec-sheet edit and a pattern change are different events, and the 0.5 cm delta is smaller than
the spread between measured Christie's lots of the same era. State it as "Chanel's published
dimensions changed in June 2020", never as "the bag got smaller in 2020".

## 4. Why sources disagree — the measurement-convention problem

This is the single largest source of apparent conflict and it needs to be stored as metadata, not
argued away.

1. **Base length versus overall width.** Rebag measures the **base**: Small 8 in, Medium 10 in,
   Jumbo/Large 12 in, Maxi 14 in. Its Small base of 20.3 cm sits 2.7 cm under the 23 cm overall
   width everyone else publishes. Any figure sourced from Rebag's 101 guide is a *base* figure and
   is not comparable to a Xupes or Christie's figure.
2. **Axis order is inconsistent and often mislabelled.** Christie's publishes **w x h x d**.
   Xupes publishes W x H x D. Miss Bugis publishes **L x H x W**, so its third number is depth, not
   width. Saclab's prose says "width x depth x height" while its table plainly reads width, height,
   depth — the source contradicts itself. If you ingest a size string without knowing the axis
   order, you will swap height and depth on roughly a third of the rows.
3. **Widest point versus flat measurement.** The 30 versus 32 cm Jumbo split and the 33 versus
   34 cm Maxi split are both about 2 cm, which is roughly the flare of a quilted flap over its base.
   No source I found states which point it measures. This is an unresolved convention gap, flagged
   rather than guessed.
4. **Material changes the number.** A denim, tweed or shearling body measures larger than the same
   pattern in caviar. The 1996-97 denim Medium at 26 x 17 x 7 is probably this rather than a design
   change, but I cannot prove it.
5. **Christie's grades and measures the object in hand.** Its figures vary lot to lot within the
   same era (Jumbos at 30 x 19 x 8.5, 30 x 20 x 9, and 30 x 20 x 10 are all "modern"). That is real
   manufacturing and measurement variance of about 1 to 1.5 cm, and it sets the floor on how precise
   any era claim can be.

**Recommendation for the catalog:** store dimensions as a **range with a convention tag**
(`overall` / `base` / `unknown`) and a `measured_by` field, not as a point value. A single number
with no convention is the thing that makes our size data ambiguous, more than the era problem does.

## 6. Section D — how reliably can a size word be resolved to an era?

**Short answer: not at all from the word, and only partly from a measurement.**

| Size | Era-resolvable from dimensions alone? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Medium / M/L** | **No.** | 25–26 W and 15–17 H across 1996 to 2026, with no trend. The variation between two lots of the same year is as large as the variation between decades. |
| **Small** | **No.** | 23 cm wide from 1997 to today. Height and depth vary 6 mm, which is noise. |
| **Jumbo / Large** | **Partly.** | Vintage 31 x 22 versus modern 30 x 20. The ~2 cm height difference is real and measurable, but only if the measurement is a genuine one. A guide-copied "30 x 20 x 10" tells you nothing. |
| **Maxi** | **Yes, coarsely.** | Vintage 36 x 22 versus modern 33–34 x 23–24. A 2–3 cm width gap plus an inverted width/height relationship. The clearest dimensional era signal on the bag. |
| **Mini / Extra Mini** | **No.** | Sources disagree with each other by more than any documented era change. |

**What actually resolves an era, in priority order:**

1. **The serial number / hologram sticker.** Deterministic, and Chanel's own. Everything else is a
   fallback. (We already hold the decode path via the Fashionphile and Miss Bugis serial guides in
   `seasonal-archive/chanel.md`.)
2. **Flap count on a Jumbo or Maxi.** Single flap means pre-2010, double flap means 2010 or later.
   Binary, sourced, and visible in almost every listing photo.
3. **Hardware.** 24k gold plating ends 2008. Silver on the Jumbo starts 1999. Oversized CC turnlock
   means pre-1998 on a Jumbo; washer-style screw-in grommets mean the 1990s.
4. **Quilt type on a Jumbo.** Vertical Mademoiselle quilting on a Jumbo points to 1993–1994.
5. **Dimensions.** Last, and only for the Jumbo and Maxi, and only from a measured source.

**The operational conclusion for our catalog.** Do not attempt to infer an era from a size word or
from a listing's stated dimensions. Instead:

- **Alias, do not split.** "Large" = "Jumbo" = "Grand Sac Classique". "Medium" = "M/L" =
  "Sac Classique" = an unqualified "Classic". Any catalog that has these as separate size rows is
  double-counting one bag. This is the highest-value immediate fix in this document.
- **Add a `flap_count` attribute** to Jumbo and Maxi variants. It is a cleaner era gate than any
  dimension and it is derivable from listing photos and from most reseller titles (Christie's,
  Fashionphile and Yoogi's all write "single flap" or "double flap" explicitly, per
  `chanel-flap-reseller-decode.md`).
- **Store dimensions as a range plus a convention tag**, and mark the source as `measured` versus
  `guide`. Treat only `measured` figures as evidence.
- **Never present a single dimension as Chanel's spec.** Chanel does not publish a historical one,
  and the current one has changed on its own website within living memory.
