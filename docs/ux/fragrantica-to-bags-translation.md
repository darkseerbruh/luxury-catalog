# Fragrantica mechanics → bags: the full translation

*2026-07-26. Every mechanic observed on the Fragrantica perfume page
(`fragrantica-teardown-0726.md`), with multiple bag translations each. Options,
not decisions. Ranked within each section by my read of leverage.*

---

## 1. Main accords → bag character bars

*Theirs: derived, proportional, ranked, colour-coded, at the very top.*

**A. Silhouette accords (recommended).** Derived from our attributes:
`structured · slouchy · compact · logo-forward · minimal · vintage · evening`.
Proportional bars, the at-a-glance identity of the bag.

**B. Material accords.** `caviar · lambskin · canvas · exotic · hardware-heavy`.
Useful because material drives most of the ownership experience.

**C. "Reads as" accords.** The cultural read: `quiet · status · workhorse ·
collector · trend`. Richer, but needs an editorial source.

**Moves:** at-a-glance comprehension → session depth. All three are derived, so no ask.

---

## 2. RATING as sentiment → bag-native emotional scale

*Theirs: love / like / ok / dislike / hate, with negativity holding two of five slots.*

**A. Bag-native wording (recommended).**
`obsessed · love it · it's fine · disappointed · sold it`
**"Sold it" is the strongest negative anchor available to us**, because it is
behavioural rather than emotional. Someone who sold a bag is telling you something
a star rating cannot.

**B. Keep it plain.** `love · like · ok · dislike · regret`.

**C. Buy-again framing.** `would buy again · probably · unsure · probably not ·
never again`. Sharper than stars, and it sidesteps the price contamination we found
in worth-it.

**Moves:** contribution rate (one tap) + honest social proof.

---

## 3. WHEN TO WEAR → when you actually carry it

*Theirs: seasons plus day/night, non-exclusive, shown as bars.*

**A. Season + occasion (recommended).** Folds in our occasion chips:
`spring · summer · fall · winter` and `everyday · work · evening · travel · special`.
Seasons are genuinely real for bags (straw, suede, white leather).

**B. Add weather tolerance.** `rain · snow · beach · city`. You flagged this
directly, and it is a real anxiety the research confirmed.

**C. Add a "leave it home" bucket.** Where owners deliberately do not take it:
`concerts · travel · rain · anywhere with kids`. Honest, and unusually useful.

**Moves:** powers "best for X" boards → SEO landing pages → affiliate.

---

## 4. What People Say → votable Reputation claims ⭐

*Theirs: AI pros and cons from member reviews, each thumb-votable, up to 1.6k votes
per line. Their highest-engagement mechanic.*

**A. Reputation claims, thumbed (strongly recommended).**
> 👍 1,204 👎 209 · *Owners say the double flap is slow to open one-handed*
> 👍 933 👎 181 · *Owners say the lambskin holds up better than expected*

Seeded from the web synthesis, then ratified or rejected by owners. **This is our
cold-start fix and our highest-leverage engagement mechanic in one.**

**B. Split by source.** Two columns, `what the web says` and `what owners here say`,
so the divergence is visible.

**C. Claim categories.** Group into `living with it · how it ages · value · style`
so the list scans.

**D. "Still true?" decay.** Let owners mark a claim outdated (a 2019 claim about a
bag that has since been reissued). Nobody does this, and reputation drifts.

**Moves:** UGC volume (the moat) + session depth + a page that works on day one.

---

## 5. Vote on the spec → crowd-verified catalog

*Theirs: notes voted on and sized by vote weight, correcting the house's own claim.*

**A. Attribute confirmation (recommended).** "Is this caviar or lambskin?"
"Gold or light gold hardware?" Sized or weighted by agreement. **This directly feeds
our catalog quality problem**, where colour is 51% covered and hardware 27%.

**B. Variant matching.** Show a listing photo and ask "which variant is this?"
Turns our unmatched-listing backlog into a game.

**C. Anatomy diagram.** A non-logo schematic of the bag with votable parts
(closure, feet, pocket, strap attachment). Visual, and it teaches while it collects.

**Moves:** catalog completeness, which gates every derived scale.

---

## 6. PERFORMANCE → the bag equivalent is almost literal

*Theirs: longevity and sillage, slider plus distribution.*

**A. Wear life ← longevity (recommended).** `shows every mark ↔ ages beautifully`.
Our `holds_up` scale, already agreed.

**B. Presence ← sillage (recommended).** Sillage is *how far the scent projects*.
The bag translation is **how far the bag projects**: `IYKYK ↔ recognisable`. This is
a genuinely tight analogue, and it argues the scale is natural rather than forced.

**C. Add "hold up under load".** Does it sag when full, do the corners go first.
Distinct from ageing on a shelf.

**Moves:** the two most-discussed lived properties in our research.

---

## 7. DEMOGRAPHICS & VALUE → four strong bag axes

*Theirs: gender as a spectrum, and price value with a real negative end.*

**A. Price value (strongly recommended).**
`way overpriced · overpriced · fair · good value · great value`
Their data proves this is **not** a restatement of the star rating: 3.76/5 overall,
yet 5.5k say way overpriced and 399 say great value. Reverses my earlier call to cut
worth-it, which failed because ours was a boolean.

**B. Retail vs preloved value (bag-native, no perfume equivalent).**
`only worth it preloved ↔ worth full retail`
This is the single most useful value question in our category and nobody asks it.

**C. Age read.** `reads young ↔ reads timeless ↔ reads mature`. You raised this
yourself with "the Classic Flap can feel very grandma". Real, and currently homeless.

**D. Gender read.** `feminine ↔ unisex ↔ masculine`. Real for bags, and it opens a
badly-served audience.

**Moves:** value questions drive the purchase decision → affiliate clicks.

---

## 8. "Reminds me of" → the crowd-built alternatives graph ⭐

*Theirs: crowd-suggested similars, thumb-ranked, with Compare on each. Contested
comparisons stay visible (Ariana Grande Cloud at 3.3k up, 1.6k down).*

**A. "This bag reminds me of" (strongly recommended).** Suggest plus vote plus
Compare. **Every card is an affiliate path**, and it answers the question shoppers
actually ask.

**B. Split the intent into three rails.**
- *The affordable alternative* — the dupe question, asked constantly, answered nowhere trustworthy
- *The same energy* — different house, similar feel
- *The upgrade* — what people move to next

**C. "I chose this over…"** Captures the actual decision, not just resemblance.
Genuinely novel, and it produces comparison content nobody has.

**D. Vintage-vs-current pairing.** Which era of the same bag people prefer.

**Moves:** discovery → affiliate, and it is evergreen SEO ("Chanel 19 dupes").

---

## 9. Collaborative recs → we already hold the graph

**A. "Collectors who have this also want…"** Straight from `closet_item`
co-occurrence. Already computable.

**B. "People who sold this bought…"** Churn-aware, and unusually honest.

**C. "Complete the rotation."** What pairs with what someone already owns, rather
than what merely resembles it.

---

## 10. Ownership shelf → ours, but visible to everyone

*Theirs: I have it 41.5K / I had it 10.6K / I want it 30.5K. **Hidden from
logged-out visitors**, which wastes the best social proof on the page.*

**A. Show counts to everyone, gate only the action (strongly recommended).** We
already have want/have/had. The fix is purely who can see it.

**B. Add "tried it".** Held it in a store but never owned it. Lets a huge group
contribute honestly, and it is exactly the group deciding whether to buy.

**C. Add "returned it".** Brutal, honest, and a strong signal.

**D. Surface the have-to-want ratio.** A bag many want and few own reads very
differently from the reverse. That is our Most Coveted board, per bag.

**Moves:** social proof for the exact visitor it should persuade.

---

## 11. Currently wearing + calendar → the carry log ⭐

*Theirs: a dated scent diary with a calendar. The Letterboxd mechanic.*

**A. "Carrying today" (strongly recommended).** One tap from the bag page or the closet.

**B. Auto cost-per-wear.** A dated log means we can compute it for free:
*"You have carried this 34 times. That is $61 a wear so far."*
**Cost per wear was the native value metric in the mid-tier research**, and a carry
log answers it without a single question asked.

**C. Rotation stats.** Which bags actually get used, which sit. Nobody holds this
data, and it is the honest counterweight to "I need another bag."

**D. Year in Bags.** The Letterboxd recap, already flagged in the swipe file.
Shareable, and a synchronised annual viral moment.

**E. Seasonality, aggregated.** Once enough logs exist, real seasonal curves per bag
rather than opinions about seasons.

**Moves:** return visits (a daily-tap habit) + genuinely unique data.

## 15. The signup gate, improved

*Theirs arrives at the moment of intent, which is right, but never says what you get.*

**Ours names the payoff:**
> **Add your read**
> You will be able to rate how bags actually wear, keep a closet of what you own
> and want, log what you carry, and get told when a bag you want drops in price.

**Moves:** signup conversion, straightforwardly.

---

## 16. Bag-native ideas with no perfume equivalent

**A. Repair log.** Did you get it repaired, where, what did it cost. Feeds the
brand-level repairability question we parked, with real numbers.

**B. "What fits" photo.** Your actual contents laid out. Answers the top-discussed
dimension visually, and it is a photo prompt with a clear brief.

**C. Authentication status.** Links each owned bag to `/identify`.

**D. Purchase channel.** Boutique, preloved, consignment, gift. The context that
makes price-value readable.

**E. Wear-and-tear photo timeline.** Photos of the same bag at year 1, 3, 5.
Nothing on the internet has this, and it is the visual proof behind our ageing scale.
